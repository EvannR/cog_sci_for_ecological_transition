# Communication climatique : méta-analyse de 71 études

## Contenu du message

### @@1 — 18:47

> "State of the art"
>
> The encouraging news: climate communication works. Across 71 studies, communication interventions had a small but robust positive effect on climate-relevant intentions, actual behavior, and support for climate policies (Hedges' g = 0.13).
>
> But how we communicate matters. The key takeaways:
>
> • Facts alone are not enough. Scientific evidence and fact-based messages had positive effects, but were outperformed by several other approaches.
> • Engage emotions and values. Messages highlighting moral and ethical considerations and those evoking awe and the beauty of nature were particularly effective. Narratives and storytelling also performed better than purely fact-based communication.
> • Help people know what to do. Providing concrete guidance toward specific climate actions was more effective than simply providing scientific information.
> • Show, don't just tell. Communication using images was more effective than text-only messages.
> • Simply emphasizing individual responsibility may not work. This was the only strategy for which the meta-analysis did not find a positive effect.
>
> The broader message is that effective climate communication needs to go beyond transmitting knowledge. Facts matter-but connecting them to emotions, values, compelling stories, and concrete possibilities for action may be key to turning information into action.
