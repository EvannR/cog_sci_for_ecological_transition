# Taxe carbone + label d'impact : des effets individuels additifs aux effets systémiques variables

## Contenu du message

There is broad consensus that we need policy mixes to accelerate the energy transition. But how does the combination of different policy interventions actually influence behaviour, and how do these effects scale from individual decisions to societal outcomes?

[The authors] looked at how combining a carbon tax with environmental impact information influences electric vehicle adoption and public support for policies.

Our initial question sounds quite simple: what happens when people are exposed to both interventions at the same time? But of course, people don't decide whether or not to buy an electric vehicle without considering what others around them are doing, how the technology is developing, how prices are changing, and which policies come and go. So instead of only focusing on individual decisions in isolation, we combined online experiments with an agent-based simulation model. This way we could study how carbon tax and information intervention influence decision-making (including attention allocation during decisions) and how these many individual decisions add up across society over time.

**Key findings:**

- Looking at the individual level, the two interventions competed for people's attention during decision-making but had **additive effects** on EV adoption choices.
- Policy mixes including the information intervention (i.e., a climate impact label) **increased public support** compared to a carbon tax alone.
- Zooming out, at the system level, things got more interesting. Depending on the country (UK, USA, South Africa, Mexico) and stage of the transition to e-mobility, the same policy mix had **additive, sub-additive, or even synergistic effects** on EV adoption.

In other words, the "simple" effects on individual behaviour translated into different system-level outcomes when accounting for differences between people, social interactions, and technological change in the agent-based model.
