## Contenu du message

Do carbon food labels change what ends up in the shopping basket?

In a pre-registered randomized field-embedded survey experiment combined with transaction-level purchasing data from over 1,600 shoppers at a major retailer, the authors find that raising consumer awareness of a carbon food label does affect purchasing behavior, but effects are modest and heterogeneous across food categories.

Rather than inducing cross-category shifts away from meat toward plant-based foods, the label primarily guides within-category choices: increased purchases of labeled convenience products, a partial reallocation within meat consumption, but no reduction in overall meat purchases. The cross-category substitutions that would meaningfully reduce dietary carbon footprints were not observed.

These findings suggest that carbon food labels alone are unlikely to bridge the intention-behavior gap in supermarket settings and should be embedded within broader policy mixes combining choice architecture, pricing instruments, and structural shifts in food environments.
