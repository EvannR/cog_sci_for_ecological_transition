**Reflections on behavioral science and climate policy** (Kristian Steensen Nielsen)

For a few years, I have critically reflected on the purpose of my own research and how we can improve behavioral science around climate change and actually promote real societal change.

Obviously, there is much to say, but I now firmly believe a core objective should be to build better knowledge and data infrastructure. Here are a few reasons why.

**1. Behavioral, psychological, and social data is too poor and fragmented**

Much of our knowledge on climate-relevant behavior builds on studies using behavioral proxies or self-reports. We all know the limitations, but we often end up there because better, more objective data is hard to come by.

At least in some countries, the data exists; it's just often limited in scope, fragmented, or owned by private companies. But this can, in principle, be solved if we build an ambitious and integrative data infrastructure together, because we need this data to deliver high-quality insights into the causes of behavior and which interventions work for which people, behaviors, and contexts.

Exactly the same could be said of psychological and social data: which barriers do people experience to adopting climate-friendly behavior, what are their social and physical circumstances, which policies do they support, and how does this vary across people and contexts?

**2. Climate policy is neither democratic nor designed to improve well-being**

Much research shows the inequality in who affects climate policy at every societal level. Danish climate policy, for example, has disproportionately focused on technological solutions with little priority given to demand-focused solutions (e.g., changing diets, reducing air travel).

However, another reason is the lack of data infrastructure, which makes it harder for policymakers and public institutions to work with.

## Rattachement à la revue

Partie 1 → **3. Les critiques encore non adressées et les perspectives**. Critique venue de l'intérieur du champ (autocritique d'un chercheur du domaine), ce qui la rend précieuse pour « rendre les critiques constructives ». Deux angles distincts à ne pas confondre : (a) un problème **d'infrastructure de données** (proxies et auto-déclaratif faute de mieux), (b) un problème **politique** (qui décide, et pour quel objectif — le bien-être n'est pas la fonction objectif).

Le point (b) est le plus proche de la conclusion visée de la Partie 1 : sans lui, on optimise des interventions dans un cadre dont on n'interroge pas la finalité — c'est-à-dire le « retour à la case départ ».
